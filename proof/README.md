# Lean proof base

This isolated Lake project records the checked roots of the [lightweight proof DAG](../docs/verification/LEAN-PROOF-DAG.md). Lean v4.33.1 and the committed `lake-manifest.json` pin Mathlib and its dependencies.

- **E0 / E2:** The quadratic orbit is defined over any commutative ring. Mathlib's `Function.minimalPeriod` gives an exact-period criterion for every positive candidate: exact return plus no return at a prime quotient, a positive proper divisor, or any earlier positive iterate. Closure bundled with either quotient test is the unconditional form. The critical-orbit specialization matches the catalog's mathematical divisor condition.
- **E1:** Mathlib's formal polynomial derivative establishes D₀ = 1, Dₙ₊₁ = 2zₙDₙ in the seed variable and Bₙ₊₁ = 2zₙBₙ + 1 in the parameter variable. The seed derivative evaluates to ∏*{k<n} 2zₖ. A varying initial seed contributes its own B₀; a constant seed gives B₀ = 0 and Bₙ equal to the sum of the truncated products ∏*{t<n−k−1} 2z_{k+1+t}. On a closed period-n orbit, that n-step seed derivative is the same at every cycle point.
- **D0 period-one field:** The multiplier chart `lam ↦ lam/2−lam²/4` has an attracting fixed point for `‖lam‖<1`, an exact quadratic radial remainder, and an upper bound on distance to its unit-multiplier curve. This does not identify that curve with the Mandelbrot boundary or give a lower distance bound.
- **F0 parabolic interval:** At `c=1/4`, the reciprocal coordinate about `z=1/2` has exact return `u↦u+1+1/(u−1)`. On real `u>1`, its iterates grow at least linearly and the quadratic orbit converges to the fixed point within `1/(u+n)`. A complex Fatou coordinate remains open.
- **Concrete L1 trap:** At `c=−1`, a radius `1/16` disk around the critical point is a primitive period-two return trap with contraction bound `1/2`, proved from explicit multiplier and divisor-separation bounds.
- **N0 restricted return:** The chart `w ↦ −w/2` conjugates the exact two-step return near `c=−1` to an explicit quartic. Its closed half-unit disk is invariant for `‖c+1‖≤1/64`. Polynomial-like properness and straightening remain open.
- **X0 exterior escape:** Outside the explicit radius `‖c‖+2`, norms at least double. Two-sided square-growth bounds make the normalized logarithmic orbit norm a Cauchy sequence, including after a finite critical-orbit entry. External angles and machine entry certificates remain open.
- **R0 selected real slice:** A real fixed point is attracting for `−3/4<c<1/4`; an exact period-two orbit is attracting for `−5/4<c<−3/4` with multiplier `4(c+1)`. This does not establish a general Sharkovsky ordering.
- **Exact rational orbit bridge:** Rational coordinate iteration embeds into the complex quadratic, and its exact rational residual and multiplier squares equal the corresponding complex squared moduli. A positive attraction cutoff can be tested by comparing the rational multiplier square with its squared cutoff. It is not a floating point refinement.
- **S0 critical period three:** Exact period-three critical centers are precisely the roots of `c³+2c²+c+1`; the extra third-return root `c=0` has period one. The cubic roots are simple. This is a center locus, not the full component boundary.
- **M1 logistic chart:** For nonzero complex `a`, the affine chart `w ↦ a(1/2−w)` conjugates `a w(1−w)` to `z² + (a/2−a²/4)`. The checked orbit, minimal-period, and formal seed-derivative theorems transport the full complex multiplier. The nondegenerate logistic parameters `a` and `2−a` have the same canonical parameter and corresponding periods and multipliers.
- **M0:** Orbit iteration commutes with ring homomorphisms, injective ring homomorphisms preserve minimal period, and bijective changes of coordinates preserve the minimal period of the transported map. Complex conjugation therefore takes the orbit, exact period, seed derivative, constant-seed parameter derivative, and both squared magnitudes at `(c,z)` to those at `(conj c, conj z)`. The sign chart is Mathlib's negation permutation and transports the map to `w ↦ −w²−c`.
- **Perturbation:** For one seed, the orbit difference eₙ at parameters `c + δ` and `c` satisfies e₀ = 0 and eₙ₊₁ = 2zₙeₙ + eₙ² + δ in any commutative ring. Over ℂ, the modulus obeys the same triangle inequality, and `‖eₙ‖` is at most the real comparison sequence `perturbationBound` whenever the reference orbit stays inside a stated radius and `‖δ‖` stays inside a stated bound. A second seed contributes its own initial gap. This comparison does not model binary64 rounding.
- **P3 glitch gate:** `gatedReference` accepts an exact-rational verifier result only for a clean or repaired orbit with an independent error-budget closure margin at the selected period. Under supplied local residuals, acceptance bounds exact closure by the stated tolerance; an unresolved raw acceptance preserves the prior record. TypeScript refinement remains open.
- **P2 / P3 error budgets:** Rebases compose, reverse, and preserve the represented state and parameter; resumed segments retain the absolute orbit index. `errorBudget` allows a separate radius and residual allowance at every step. `inexactOrbit_error_le_budget` propagates caller-supplied local errors; `reconstruction_error_le_budget` separates reference-step, delta-step, and parameter-offset errors. `rebase_error_norm_le` accounts for conversion error during a rounded rebase. `orbit_norm_sub_le_budget` supplies a uniform orbit enclosure for a norm-bounded parameter/seed region. An approximate critical iterate whose disk margin includes its budget supplies L1 entry through `existsUnique_critical_return_of_error_budget`. These conditional contracts do not derive binary64 residual bounds or a renderer glitch policy. See the [batch contracts and next obligations](../docs/verification/LEAN-ERROR-BUDGETS.md).
- **J0 derivative budgets:** A finite orbit-radius prefix bounds the formal return multiplier and the constant-seed parameter derivative by explicit recurrences. `region_derivatives_le_budget` combines those recurrences with `orbit_norm_sub_le_budget`, giving the same bounds for every parameter and seed within supplied norm distances of a reference. The caller must still certify the reference radii and all outward rounding; this is not an interval-arithmetic implementation.
- **P4 parameter jets (partial):** Taylor/Hasse coefficients, all-order convolution, and exact truncation hold over any commutative ring. Recursive caps and retained-product residuals give P3 error bounds without full discarded-tail caps. `parameterJetApproximation_error_le_enclosure` checks finite outward coefficient, shift, and error tables. A third-order certificate at parameter/seed zero gives error at most `1/10000` at iterate four on `‖δ‖≤1/16`; a nonzero reference at parameter minus one gives error at most `1/1000000` at every iterate through sixteen on `‖δ‖≤1/256`, with critical seed zero. Separate multiply/add Horner operation error, inclusive decoded coefficient error, and exact truncation caps now compose conditionally; offset mismatch has a separate P3 budget. The minus-one `1/1000000` cap is preserved if each retained coefficient and local operation error is at most `2⁻⁴⁰`, with the same decoded target offset. Scalar primitive contracts now follow four separate products and component subtraction/addition; finite retained generation propagates inclusive coefficient errors with product cross terms. An executable unbounded-integer dyadic reference has proved generation/Horner decoding correspondence, multiplication residual at most `4·2⁻ᵖ`, and exact grid addition. Copied real integer anchors and seeds have exact retained coefficients at every precision and horizon. `minusOneDyadicJetEvaluate_error_le` proves total error at most `1/1000000` for the executable 42-bit fractional-grid minus-one generator/evaluator at order three through iterate sixteen on `‖decode₄₂(δ)‖≤1/256`, with no external coefficient or primitive-accuracy hypotheses. A [bounded numerical pilot](../docs/verification/NUMERICAL-KERNEL-PILOT.md) uses a Lean-checked exact coefficient packet and per-call BigInt disk/residual checks for three binary64 Horner steps; its JavaScript checker remains trusted/tested. Full binary64/TypeScript refinement and measured integration remain open; [batch notes](../docs/verification/PARAMETER-JETS.md) state the boundaries.
- **Branch:** With the seed fixed, the parameter derivative of the orbit is Bₙ. With the parameter fixed, the seed derivative is the multiplier λ. A moving seed has total derivative `λ φ' + B`. A differentiable local branch of a period-n point therefore has slope `B / (1 - λ)` when `λ ≠ 1`, and `1 - λ` is the same at every point of the cycle. When `λ ≠ 1`, `exists_branch_slope` gives a local graph of exact period-n points over the parameter, with that slope. The graph is the kernel section of `(λ - 1) dz + B dc` reparameterized by `c`. This does not certify `guardDisplacement = 0.01` or the Newton floor `1e-12`.
- **Return disk:** If the formal multiplier of an n-step quadratic return is at most `q < 1` on a closed disk, and the center moves by at most the slack `r - q r`, the disk contains a unique fixed point and that point's multiplier is at most `q`. This does not derive the trap constants, and it does not say that the critical orbit enters the disk.
- **Primitive critical entry:** A derivative bound on each proper-divisor return disk plus a strict center-image separation excludes all shorter returns. `existsUnique_primitive_critical_return_of_error_budget` combines these tests with certified critical entry, proving the attracting return point has minimal period `n`. The required numerical disk and divisor certificates remain inputs.
- **Critical entry:** If some critical iterate `orbit c k 0` already lies in that disk, later iterates `orbit c (k + m n) 0` stay in the disk and approach its unique return fixed point at rate `q ^ m`. Entry itself remains a hypothesis. The return fixed point has exact period `n` only when no positive proper divisor returns. This does not give the critical point period `n`, and it does not derive `minLambda = 0.8` or `diskFactor = 4`.
- **Rebase:** For one seed and an arbitrary iterate, `orbit c n z = orbit c₁ n z + perturbation c₁ (c - c₁) z n`. Changing the reference from `c₀` to `c₁` replaces the perturbation by `dₙ - (orbit c₁ n z - orbit c₀ n z)`. These are ring equalities. They do not bound a glitch or a rounding error.
- **Fixed-disk guard:** A uniform derivative-variation or second-derivative bound on a parameter disk gives an explicit predictor remainder and actual branch-displacement bound. `displacementGuard_crosses_parabolic_parameter` proves that the frozen `0.01` first-order attempt guard alone can cross the real period-one boundary; `real_above_quarter_fixedPoint_repelling` confirms the destination has no attracting fixed point. Concrete disk constants and binary64 refinement remain open.
- **Guard:** When `λ ≠ 1`, the first-order predictor displacement has modulus `‖B‖ * ‖δ‖ / ‖1 - λ‖`, and a positive lower bound `α ≤ ‖1 - λ‖` replaces the denominator by `α`. The local periodic graph makes that predictor the derivative, so the remainder is little-o of `δ`. This does not certify `guardDisplacement = 0.01`, the Newton floor `1e-12`, or that the corrected seed is periodic.
- **J0 / L0 / primitive disk:** `diskOrbitError` and `diskOrbitRadius` enclose every intermediate orbit over a parameter/seed disk. `multiplierBound` encloses the formal seed derivative, supplying an explicit sufficient return-disk certificate through `existsUnique_fixedPoint_orbit_of_disk_enclosure`. Strict proper-divisor separations give primitive period throughout the region; a reference critical-entry margin then gives critical-return contraction. The period-two example near `c = -1` certifies a parameter disk of radius `1/256`, seed radius `1/16`, and contraction bound `129/512`. Exact real inequalities still need certified outward evaluation. See the [disk-certificate contracts](../docs/verification/LEAN-DISK-CERTIFICATES.md).
- **J0 / G2 / J1 continuation:** `parameterDerivativeBound` propagates `T₀ = 0`, `Tₙ₊₁ = 2RₙTₙ + 1` from enclosed orbit radii, bounding fixed-seed parameter partials and finite parameter changes over the convex disk. A uniform multiplier bound `Qₙ < 1` supplies denominator margin `1 - Qₙ`, slope bound `Tₙ / (1 - Qₙ)`, and a linear proposal displacement bound. Two exact return points in the common disks satisfy the same finite movement constant times their parameter gap. The period-two example checks `T₂ = 193/64`, denominator margin `383/512`, and movement constant `1544/383`. This finite root estimate does not supply a second-order predictor remainder or certify machine arithmetic. See the [continuation contracts](../docs/verification/LEAN-PARAMETER-ENCLOSURES.md).
- **P3 / J0 stored references:** `storedOrbitError` uses radii of stored values and forcing `Δ + residual k`, including both parameter uncertainty and local reference-step errors. `orbit_sub_storedReference_le_budget` encloses exact target orbits without exact-reference radius inputs; its segment form preserves absolute indices after a checkpoint or rebase. `StoredDisk` carries these enclosures into both formal derivatives, denominator/slope bounds, invariant return disks, primitive-period exclusions, and critical entry. A deliberately biased period-two prefix with positive initial error and nonzero step residuals certifies a parameter disk of radius `1/512` with multiplier at most `1/3`. Supplying certified backend error bounds and evaluating comparisons outward remain open. See the [stored-reference contracts](../docs/verification/LEAN-STORED-REFERENCES.md).
- **J0 / J2 outward rational grid:** `gridFloor` and `gridCeil` round endpoints outward on a positive rational grid, with error below one spacing in each direction. `roundedBoxOrbit` and `roundedBoxMultiplier` retain sound enclosures after every rounded step. Computed residual boxes and `rationalStoredError_cast` discharge a rational arithmetic slice of the stored-reference contracts. `certifyRoundedCriticalPeriodTwo` checks closure/divisor/multiplier margins before accepting a tile; it certifies the existing tile at spacing `2^-64` and explicitly refuses it at spacing one. This is an executable arbitrary-size rational backend, with binary64/TypeScript refinement still open. See the [outward-grid contracts](../docs/verification/LEAN-OUTWARD-GRID.md).
- **J2 reflected tile checker:** `checkTile` is one executable checker, generic over period, critical-entry index, dyadic precision, and parameter/seed disks. It only verifies integer witnesses (stored reference values, radius/residual witnesses, seed and center error tables, a multiplier chain, and a stored critical prefix) with `Int`/`Nat` `+ − ×` comparisons, so instances are discharged by `decide +kernel` with no trusted emitter. `checkTile_sound` proves once, via the stored-reference contracts, that acceptance gives every parameter in the closed decoded parameter disk a point `ζ` of the seed disk that is the only fixed point of the `p`-step return there, has exact minimal period `p` and multiplier at most the decoded `q < 1`, and to which the critical orbit returns at iterates `k + m·p`, contracting at rate `q^m`; `checkTile_sound_rectangle` covers rectangles with `a²+b² ≤ Δ²`. The optional `checkTileMultiplierLower` adds lower radius witnesses and a lower product chain; `checkTile_sound_multiplier_bounds` gives a two-sided multiplier enclosure. Kernel-checked instances: the `−1` period-two tile (recovering `periodTwoRationalTile_complex_trap`'s constants), a period-three tile near the rabbit center, and a non-real period-three tile near the rabbit-component boundary (`c ≈ −0.05344 + 0.69732i`, parameter radius `2⁻²⁰`, critical entry at iterate 150) with `0.89 ≤ ‖λ‖ ≤ 0.91`, plus four refusals. Witnesses and batch modules come from the untrusted BigInt emitter `tools/emit_tile_certificate.ts`. Uniqueness is only within the seed disk; binary64 refinement and a subdivision driver remain open.
- **Fast paths:** If `s * s = 1 - 4c`, then `(1 - s) / 2` is fixed and its multiplier is `1 - s`. A root of `z² + z + (c + 1) = 0` has multiplier `4(c + 1)`, and when `c ≠ -3/4` its minimal period is 2. `‖4(c + 1)‖ < 1` if and only if `‖c + 1‖ < 1/4`, and that open bulb excludes `c = -3/4`. For a square root with nonnegative real part, `‖1 - s‖ < 1` is the cardioid inequality `q(q + (x - 1/4)) < y²/4`. These identities do not certify `Math.hypot`, `Math.sqrt`, or the attraction margin.
- **Concrete rational J0/J1 tile:** Exact rational boxes produce finite orbit, derivative, and lower/upper squared-norm bounds over a whole rectangle. A tiny critical-seed rectangle around `c=−1` receives a uniform accepted period-two verdict from both exact rational verifier procedures at the unit-scale policy thresholds. Every complex parameter in this tile also lies in the certified L1 neighborhood, so its critical orbit reaches a unique attracting primitive period-two return point. A checked rational counterexample in the tile is accepted by the model while lacking exact critical period two; the tile does not refine binary64.
- **Bounded verifier correctness pilot:** `periodTwoVerifierPilot_residual_margins` strengthens the existing period-two tile margins; `periodTwoVerifierPilot_audited_accepts` proves full-record V0/V1 acceptance from per-frame residual-square discrepancy checks and decoded policy margins. A [research-only executable certificate](../docs/verification/PERIOD-TWO-VERIFIER-PILOT.md) audits the critical-seed candidate/divisor arithmetic and requires production verifier agreement. JavaScript decoding/checking and source correspondence remain trusted/tested. The returned critical-seed zero multiplier is not the true attracting cycle multiplier; arbitrary starts, periods, and output accuracy remain open.
- **L1 parameter neighborhood:** For every `‖c+1‖≤1/1024`, the critical orbit enters a radius `1/16` primitive period-two return trap after two steps; the return multiplier is bounded by `1/2`. Separately, `factorFourRadius_not_invariant` shows the PoC factor-four radius is not a general invariant-disk certificate.
- **J0 / J1 uniformity:** A shared orbit-error budget bounds closure-residual variation; caller-supplied rational residual and multiplier intervals that avoid each threshold yield one V0/V1 verdict and accepted period across a region. The intervals require certified outward arithmetic for a concrete rectangle.
- **V0 / V1:** Two ordered verifier procedures share one decision tree and differ only in proper-divisor reduction. On the _same_ exact rational frame prefix they yield the same verdict and entire output record. Refusals preserve the old record. `properDivisors` is strictly increasing, and a `.reduced d` result is the least proper divisor whose residual is accepted. The acceptance and exclusion thresholds are ordered abstractly, and accepted payload fields are opaque.
- **V2 frame cache:** Over an abstract deterministic state step and abstract finiteness, residual, multiplier, payload, and start-dependent threshold functions, `cachedVerify` (one recorded walk; divisor frames read from the record) returns the same verdict code and complete record as `recomputeVerify` (today's main walk plus independent ascending divisor re-walks, with `break` on the first accepted divisor). Divisor re-walk finiteness checks provably never fire; refusals preserve the record. Unless a finiteness check fires, both equal V0/V1 on the prefix frames `step^[d] s`, with an explicit accepted-record characterization. Because every lag-scan candidate starts from the same point, one truncating shared record serves all candidate periods `n ≤ N` with the same result (`sharedCachedVerify_eq_recomputeVerify`); a per-step failure beyond `n` must only truncate the record, not abort the scan. The inline end-check path has the same shared reuse under an absorbing-non-finite hypothesis. The module docstring lists the sufficient refinement constraints a TypeScript cache must preserve. Binary64 semantics, TypeScript correspondence, candidate selection, and performance are not proved.
- **Certified acceptance (V0/V1 + L0/L1, generic period):** `Verifier.reference_accepted_spec`/`inline_accepted_spec` characterise acceptance on both `.keep` and `.reduced` paths: output period `P ∣ n`, closure accepted, every proper divisor of `P` excluded, and the full record. `existsUnique_primitive_of_reference_accepted` and its inline, critical-entry, exact-reference `_disk`, stored-reference `_stored`, and critical-stored forms turn acceptance plus η-tracked residual squares, a disk multiplier bound `q<1`, and frame-value windows (`residual(P)+η ≤ (r(1−q))²`; divisor separations below `residual(e)−η`) into a primitive period-`P` return point ζ in the disk with `‖λ‖≤q` that equals every `P`-step fixed point in the disk. The `_stored` forms take only StoredDisk inequalities, ready for a reflected checker. `_thresholds` corollaries use `acceptSquared`/`excludeSquared` instead; for `P ≥ 2` they need `(1+L)/(1−q) < 100` at the frozen ratio (`thresholdWindow_ratio`), a ceiling the frame-value forms do not have. Proved instances: c=−1 (P=2 via `.keep`, and via `.reduced` from n=4), c=0 (P=1), and c=−0.75125 (P=2, |λ|≈0.995, r=1e-4), where no threshold-form certificate exists. Uniqueness holds only inside the disk (no Fatou theorem); the frame multiplier field is not certified; binary64 is covered only through a separately proved η (all instances use exact rational frames); TypeScript refinement is not proved.

The model's `Frame` uses rational residual squares and multiplier magnitudes. It does not model nonfinite input, signed zero, floating point rounding, `hypot`, angle/log output, or how the TypeScript lag scan chooses and evaluates frames. Accordingly, these proofs neither certify binary64 period/attraction nor prove the TypeScript implementation refines the Lean model. Retain the existing differential and oracle tests; a future refinement proof must specify an arithmetic error bound and finite-prefix correspondence. Similarly, conjugating a map by an arbitrary chart preserves its transported dynamics, not necessarily the canonical quadratic family or its parameter coordinates.

From this directory, with `elan` installed:

```sh
lake update
lake build
lake env lean IntMProof/Axioms.lean
lake lint
lake exe lint-style
```

`lake update` refreshes the lockfile and should be reviewed and committed deliberately; for routine reproducible checks use the committed manifest without updating. The default `lake build` selects the theorem root `IntMProof` and the aggregate `IntMProof.Axioms`, which imports all separate `*Axioms.lean` checks. The root glob keeps its submodules buildable in Lake; selecting only the audit module does not register those dependencies. This builds their imported object files and checks the guards even on a fresh checkout; `lake env lean` alone does not build missing imports. `lintDriverArgs` selects the aggregate explicitly, so Batteries lints it once, covering all imported project declarations without reloading and linting the same environment for every source file. The axiom guards check selected theorems' exact axiom sets and fail if those change, including introduction of `sorryAx`. CI also uses `lean-action`'s namespace-wide axiom audit to catch unchecked declarations. `lake lint` runs Batteries' declaration linter over the project root; `lake exe lint-style` runs Mathlib's text style linters. For a supported automatic style fix, use `lake exe lint-style --fix` and review the resulting diff. Lean/Mathlib does not supply a general semantics-preserving code formatter through these commands; follow the [Mathlib style guide](https://leanprover-community.github.io/contribute/style.html) when editing proofs. Keep declarations documented, name hypotheses and lemmas by mathematical content, prefer existing Mathlib APIs, and rerun build and axiom audit after refactoring.

Mathlib source APIs used here include [periodic points](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Dynamics/PeriodicPts/Defs.html), [polynomial derivatives](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/Derivative.html), and complex conjugation. The [Mathlib downstream lint guide](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/wiki/Setting-up-linting-and-testing-for-your-Lean-project) and [lean-action inputs](https://github.com/leanprover/lean-action/blob/main/action.yml) describe the tooling. The precise imported versions are the pinned files under `.lake/packages/mathlib` after `lake update`.
