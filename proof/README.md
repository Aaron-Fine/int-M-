# Lean proof base

This isolated Lake project records the checked roots of the [lightweight proof DAG](../docs/verification/LEAN-PROOF-DAG.md). Lean v4.33.1 and the committed `lake-manifest.json` pin Mathlib and its dependencies.

- **E0 / E2:** The quadratic orbit is defined over any commutative ring. Mathlib's `Function.minimalPeriod` gives an exact-period criterion for every positive candidate: exact return plus no return at a prime quotient, a positive proper divisor, or any earlier positive iterate. Closure bundled with either quotient test is the unconditional form. The critical-orbit specialization matches the catalog's mathematical divisor condition.
- **E1:** Mathlib's formal polynomial derivative establishes D₀ = 1, Dₙ₊₁ = 2zₙDₙ in the seed variable and Bₙ₊₁ = 2zₙBₙ + 1 in the parameter variable. The seed derivative evaluates to ∏*{k<n} 2zₖ. A varying initial seed contributes its own B₀; a constant seed gives B₀ = 0 and Bₙ equal to the sum of the truncated products ∏*{t<n−k−1} 2z_{k+1+t}. On a closed period-n orbit, that n-step seed derivative is the same at every cycle point.
- **M0:** Orbit iteration commutes with ring homomorphisms, injective ring homomorphisms preserve minimal period, and bijective changes of coordinates preserve the minimal period of the transported map. Complex conjugation therefore takes the orbit, exact period, seed derivative, constant-seed parameter derivative, and both squared magnitudes at `(c,z)` to those at `(conj c, conj z)`. The sign chart is Mathlib's negation permutation and transports the map to `w ↦ −w²−c`.
- **Perturbation:** For one seed, the orbit difference eₙ at parameters `c + δ` and `c` satisfies e₀ = 0 and eₙ₊₁ = 2zₙeₙ + eₙ² + δ in any commutative ring. Over ℂ, the modulus obeys the same triangle inequality, and `‖eₙ‖` is at most the real comparison sequence `perturbationBound` whenever the reference orbit stays inside a stated radius and `‖δ‖` stays inside a stated bound. A second seed contributes its own initial gap. This comparison does not model binary64 rounding.
- **P2 / P3 error budgets:** Rebases compose, reverse, and preserve the represented state and parameter; resumed segments retain the absolute orbit index. `errorBudget` allows a separate radius and residual allowance at every step. `inexactOrbit_error_le_budget` propagates caller-supplied local errors; `reconstruction_error_le_budget` separates reference-step, delta-step, and parameter-offset errors. `rebase_error_norm_le` accounts for conversion error during a rounded rebase. `orbit_norm_sub_le_budget` supplies a uniform orbit enclosure for a norm-bounded parameter/seed region. An approximate critical iterate whose disk margin includes its budget supplies L1 entry through `existsUnique_critical_return_of_error_budget`. These conditional contracts do not derive binary64 residual bounds or a renderer glitch policy. See the [batch contracts and next obligations](../docs/verification/LEAN-ERROR-BUDGETS.md).
- **Branch:** With the seed fixed, the parameter derivative of the orbit is Bₙ. With the parameter fixed, the seed derivative is the multiplier λ. A moving seed has total derivative `λ φ' + B`. A differentiable local branch of a period-n point therefore has slope `B / (1 - λ)` when `λ ≠ 1`, and `1 - λ` is the same at every point of the cycle. When `λ ≠ 1`, `exists_branch_slope` gives a local graph of exact period-n points over the parameter, with that slope. The graph is the kernel section of `(λ - 1) dz + B dc` reparameterized by `c`. This does not certify `guardDisplacement = 0.01` or the Newton floor `1e-12`.
- **Return disk:** If the formal multiplier of an n-step quadratic return is at most `q < 1` on a closed disk, and the center moves by at most the slack `r - q r`, the disk contains a unique fixed point and that point's multiplier is at most `q`. This does not derive the trap constants, and it does not say that the critical orbit enters the disk.
- **Critical entry:** If some critical iterate `orbit c k 0` already lies in that disk, later iterates `orbit c (k + m n) 0` stay in the disk and approach its unique return fixed point at rate `q ^ m`. Entry itself remains a hypothesis. The return fixed point has exact period `n` only when no positive proper divisor returns. This does not give the critical point period `n`, and it does not derive `minLambda = 0.8` or `diskFactor = 4`.
- **Rebase:** For one seed and an arbitrary iterate, `orbit c n z = orbit c₁ n z + perturbation c₁ (c - c₁) z n`. Changing the reference from `c₀` to `c₁` replaces the perturbation by `dₙ - (orbit c₁ n z - orbit c₀ n z)`. These are ring equalities. They do not bound a glitch or a rounding error.
- **Guard:** When `λ ≠ 1`, the first-order predictor displacement has modulus `‖B‖ * ‖δ‖ / ‖1 - λ‖`, and a positive lower bound `α ≤ ‖1 - λ‖` replaces the denominator by `α`. The local periodic graph makes that predictor the derivative, so the remainder is little-o of `δ`. This does not certify `guardDisplacement = 0.01`, the Newton floor `1e-12`, or that the corrected seed is periodic.
- **J0 / L0 / primitive disk:** `diskOrbitError` and `diskOrbitRadius` enclose every intermediate orbit over a parameter/seed disk. `multiplierBound` encloses the formal seed derivative, supplying an explicit sufficient return-disk certificate through `existsUnique_fixedPoint_orbit_of_disk_enclosure`. Strict proper-divisor separations give primitive period throughout the region; a reference critical-entry margin then gives critical-return contraction. The period-two example near `c = -1` certifies a parameter disk of radius `1/256`, seed radius `1/16`, and contraction bound `129/512`. Exact real inequalities still need certified outward evaluation. See the [disk-certificate contracts](../docs/verification/LEAN-DISK-CERTIFICATES.md).
- **J0 / G2 / J1 continuation:** `parameterDerivativeBound` propagates `T₀ = 0`, `Tₙ₊₁ = 2RₙTₙ + 1` from enclosed orbit radii, bounding fixed-seed parameter partials and finite parameter changes over the convex disk. A uniform multiplier bound `Qₙ < 1` supplies denominator margin `1 - Qₙ`, slope bound `Tₙ / (1 - Qₙ)`, and a linear proposal displacement bound. Two exact return points in the common disks satisfy the same finite movement constant times their parameter gap. The period-two example checks `T₂ = 193/64`, denominator margin `383/512`, and movement constant `1544/383`. This finite root estimate does not supply a second-order predictor remainder or certify machine arithmetic. See the [continuation contracts](../docs/verification/LEAN-PARAMETER-ENCLOSURES.md).
- **Fast paths:** If `s * s = 1 - 4c`, then `(1 - s) / 2` is fixed and its multiplier is `1 - s`. A root of `z² + z + (c + 1) = 0` has multiplier `4(c + 1)`, and when `c ≠ -3/4` its minimal period is 2. `‖4(c + 1)‖ < 1` if and only if `‖c + 1‖ < 1/4`, and that open bulb excludes `c = -3/4`. For a square root with nonnegative real part, `‖1 - s‖ < 1` is the cardioid inequality `q(q + (x - 1/4)) < y²/4`. These identities do not certify `Math.hypot`, `Math.sqrt`, or the attraction margin.
- **V0 / V1:** Two ordered verifier procedures share one decision tree and differ only in proper-divisor reduction. On the _same_ exact rational frame prefix they yield the same verdict and entire output record. Refusals preserve the old record. `properDivisors` is strictly increasing, and a `.reduced d` result is the least proper divisor whose residual is accepted. The acceptance and exclusion thresholds are ordered abstractly, and accepted payload fields are opaque.

The model's `Frame` uses rational residual squares and multiplier magnitudes. It does not model nonfinite input, signed zero, floating point rounding, `hypot`, angle/log output, or how the TypeScript lag scan chooses and evaluates frames. Accordingly, these proofs neither certify binary64 period/attraction nor prove the TypeScript implementation refines the Lean model. Retain the existing differential and oracle tests; a future refinement proof must specify an arithmetic error bound and finite-prefix correspondence. Similarly, conjugating a map by an arbitrary chart preserves its transported dynamics, not necessarily the canonical quadratic family or its parameter coordinates.

From this directory, with `elan` installed:

```sh
lake update
lake build
lake env lean IntMProof/Axioms.lean
lake lint
lake exe lint-style
```

`lake update` refreshes the lockfile and should be reviewed and committed deliberately; for routine reproducible checks use the committed manifest without updating. The axiom guards check selected theorems' exact axiom sets and fail if those change, including introduction of `sorryAx`. CI also uses `lean-action`'s namespace-wide axiom audit to catch unchecked declarations. `lake lint` runs Batteries' declaration linter over the project root; `lake exe lint-style` runs Mathlib's text style linters. For a supported automatic style fix, use `lake exe lint-style --fix` and review the resulting diff. Lean/Mathlib does not supply a general semantics-preserving code formatter through these commands; follow the [Mathlib style guide](https://leanprover-community.github.io/contribute/style.html) when editing proofs. Keep declarations documented, name hypotheses and lemmas by mathematical content, prefer existing Mathlib APIs, and rerun build and axiom audit after refactoring.

Mathlib source APIs used here include [periodic points](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Dynamics/PeriodicPts/Defs.html), [polynomial derivatives](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/Polynomial/Derivative.html), and complex conjugation. The [Mathlib downstream lint guide](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/wiki/Setting-up-linting-and-testing-for-your-Lean-project) and [lean-action inputs](https://github.com/leanprover/lean-action/blob/main/action.yml) describe the tooling. The precise imported versions are the pinned files under `.lake/packages/mathlib` after `lake update`.
