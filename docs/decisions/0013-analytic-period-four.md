# ADR 0013: Analytic period-4 classification with a certified root

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Amended: 2026-10-20 (how the kernels reach the gate and the solve, Decisions 3 and 4; see the amendment at the end)

## Context

The workstream H spike ([record](../verification/WORKSTREAM-H-SPIKE-2026-10-16.md)) solved the cycle-product cubic of the three period-4 cycles by Cardano and found that period-4 pixels dominate the hard views: in Node the prototype ran the 126×, 13× and 609× views 1.4–1.8× faster and the 6,000,000× view 8.4× faster, and it resolved the left half of that view, which no iteration budget reaches (|λ| between 1 − 5·10⁻⁶ and 1 − 2·10⁻⁸). Its acceptance bound was first order and heuristic near the cusps. Owner decisions: adopt period 3 now and period 4 only after its error bound is rigorous (2026-10-16); period 3 is accepted "like p1/p2", by the margin plus a derived allowance (2026-10-17, [ADR 0010](0010-analytic-period-three.md) Decision 2); for period 4 the bar is a rigorous bound, and a heuristic cusp bound is not acceptable. On 2026-10-17 the owner accepted the paper-proved Rouché enclosure plus the exact sampled audit as meeting that bar.

## Decision

1. A fourth analytic fast path, `classifyPeriodFourInto` (`src/domain/analytic-period-four.ts`, revision `src-analytic-p4-1.0.0`). It computes an untrusted candidate root of the cubic μ³ + (c² − 3)μ² − (c⁴ + c³ − c² − 3)μ − G₄(c) (Cardano and two Newton steps per root) and accepts only through an a-posteriori Rouché enclosure: with F ≥ |M(μ̃)|, D ≤ |M′(μ̃)|, K ≥ |M″(μ̃)|/2 for the exact cubic at the binary64 parameter and h = F/D, it requires h(8K + 16h) < D and 16|μ̃| + 64h + 2⁻⁵⁰ < 1 − attractMargin. The enclosure, the exact period and the binary64 budget are in [PERFORMANCE-MATHEMATICS §12](../PERFORMANCE-MATHEMATICS.md#12-period-4-cubic-with-a-certified-root-workstream-h-adr-0013); the exact identities are checked in `proof/IntMProof/PeriodFourMultiplier.lean`. Acceptance implies an exact root with |λ| < 1 − 10⁻¹² and a cycle of exact period 4, for every finite binary64 c (the resultant identity of §12.1, sympy-checked, then Lean: `period4_minimalPeriod_of_attracting`, which rests on `period4_not_period_two_of_attracting`).
2. Acceptance is the analytic criterion of periods 1–3 (plan §4, bucket 1–2): the verifier policy's attraction margin applied to a multiplier known to lie within a proved allowance, without `verifyCycleInto` (there is no cycle point to hand to it). This extends the owner's period-3 ruling under the owner's own condition for period 4, a rigorous bound; `MI-PERF-004`'s parenthetical names it. `VERIFIER_REVISION` does not change.
3. Placement: both kernels try it once per pixel at the first scanning iteration, `max(cycleWarmup, 2)` = 24, exactly where the period-3 closed form runs. The period-3 and period-4 boxes are disjoint, so at most one solve runs. The record depends on c alone, so placement changes no bit.
4. Gate: padded bounding boxes of the six components, folded by conjugation into four (`src/domain/period-four-gate.ts`). The gate must contain every accepted parameter; it is measured on dense grids, and every accepted parameter lies in a period-4 component by Decision 1. Originally the scalar kernel called the straight-line `inPeriodFourGate` and the row kernel evaluated it once per row as two windows on Re c (the two real-axis boxes share |Im c| values), sharing one spill block and call site per lane with period 3. Since the 2026-10-20 amendment both kernels call `classifyGatedAnalyticInto`, which reads the same two windows for the row and calls the solve.
5. Evidence: flag `analytic-period-4-component`, code 6, evidence source `analytic`. The inspector shows "Analytic Period 4 Component" and "Certified analytic solve" (owner decision 2026-10-18: the solve is not a closed form, so its source label differs from the "Analytic closed form" of periods 1–3). Iterations are 0.
6. Multiplier: period-4 pixels report λ̃ = 16μ̃, within 32h(1 + 2⁻⁴⁰) of the exact root, about half the allowance 64h + 2⁻⁵⁰; measured within 4.5·10⁻³ of the allowance of the dd cycle on the golden raster, against up to 3.2·10⁻⁶ for the orbit product.
7. Inputs with |c| > 4, NaN and ±∞ are refused before any work. Near the three primitive cusps (within about 10⁻¹²) and within the margin plus the allowance of |λ| = 1 the certificate refuses and the unchanged orbit path decides.
8. This supersedes ADR 0010 Decision 7 ("Period 4 is out of scope until its root-error bound is rigorous").

## Consequences

- Answer change (plan §3: a changed answer ships only when the oracle supports it). On the golden raster (160×100) 8,614 pixels move from unresolved to attracting period 4 (2 in the default view, 312 at 126×, 84 at 609×, 216 at 13×, and all 8,000 of the 6,000,000× view's left half), and 3,821 period-4 pixels report the certified multiplier instead of the orbit product. All 12,435 are checked against the independent dd period-4 cycle (`tests/unit/render/period-four-golden-oracle.test.ts`). At the shipping raster the 6,000,000× view's 327,680 unresolved pixels all resolve, each dd-adjudicated.
- The golden kernel fixture changes for five cases and the colorized-buffer golden for seven, in the same commit; the advisory screenshot baselines of `default-stability`, `period-four-126x-period` and `catalog-overlay` are regenerated there in both engines. `SEMANTIC_ALGORITHM_VERSION` becomes 4.
- The differential tests against the frozen legacy port and the PoC control kernel gain one documented divergence class, analytic period 4, adjudicated in `tests/unit/domain/orbit-oracle-adjudication.test.ts`; the legacy period-4 reductions (8 → 4, 12 → 4) on the stratified grid are now answered analytically, so only period-5 reductions remain there.
- Speed, measured in Node ([evidence](../../evidence/phase-2/2026-10-17-cf414cc-period-four-overhead/summary.md)): the stable pass ran the 126×, 13×, 609× and 6,000,000× views 1.72×, 1.69×, 1.42× and 7.2× faster, and the far-exterior and ambiguous-boundary views stayed within ±1% in both passes (every 95% interval; the limit is 5%). The row kernel's code generation remains the fragile point ADR 0010 records: rerun `tools/benchmark/fallback-overhead.ts`, with and without `--stride`, after any change to `classifyRow` or a V8 upgrade.
- The candidate uses `Math.cbrt`, `Math.atan2`, `Math.cos` and `Math.sin`, which ECMAScript does not require to be correctly rounded. The certificate does not depend on them, so every answer is sound in every engine, but the multiplier's last bits, and at a knife-edge pixel the decision itself, may differ between engines. The owner accepted this on 2026-10-17. Before this change status and period depended only on correctly rounded operations.
- The browser release run in both browsers is deferred to the owner's screen session.
- Evidence: [adjudication](../../evidence/phase-2/2026-10-17-cf414cc-period-four-adjudication/summary.md) (every
  changed pixel and the 6,000,000× view at 1024×640, 0 unsupported) and
  [fallback overhead and gain](../../evidence/phase-2/2026-10-17-cf414cc-period-four-overhead/summary.md)
  (Node, directional).

## Amendment 2026-10-20: one gated call per pixel

The period-4 path shares the placement change of [ADR 0010's amendment of 2026-10-20](0010-analytic-period-three.md#amendment-2026-10-20-one-gated-call-per-pixel-nothing-first-run-late-in-the-row-kernel): both kernels now call `classifyGatedAnalyticInto` for every pixel that reaches the first scanning iteration; it tries this solve when c lies in a period-4 window of its row and outside the period-3 window. The per-row windows and gated call blocks the row kernel held under Decision 4 cost cold tile workers repeated recompiles in both browsers. The solve itself, its certificate and its allowance are unchanged, and so is every answer. Measured effect on the views this path answers ([evidence](../../evidence/phase-2/2026-10-20-ab73117-analytic-cost/summary.md)): in the full harness (directional, 20 warm pairs) the 13× view is 6% (Chromium) and 11% (Firefox) faster than with `b642fd6`, 126× unchanged in Chromium and 7% faster in Firefox, and the 6,000,000× view within ±2% in both; in Node, warm, 126×, 13×, 609× and 6,000,000× moved by −2.4% to +1.0% (point estimates); the 6,000,000× interval, +1.01% [−4.23%, +5.16%], reaches just above 5%, the others stay below it.
