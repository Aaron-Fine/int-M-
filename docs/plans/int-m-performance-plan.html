1. System context

Product

Mandelbrot Interiority is a TypeScript/Vite browser atlas that visualizes internal Mandelbrot dynamics, not merely escape-time color. Each pixel may carry:

  • classification: escaped, attracting cycle, or unresolved;
  • detected primitive period;
  • multiplier magnitude and argument;
  • stability exponent κ = −log|λ| / p.

Current runtime

  • CPU binary64 orbit classifier in Web Workers.
  • Supervisor worker plus four nested tile workers.
  • Coarse preview followed by stable semantic frame.
  • Maximum raster edge 1024 (768 on the Quick profile) and zoom-product ceiling 6,000,000×.
  • Chrome and Firefox are release targets; no backend is required.

2. Current diagnosis

Primary hot path

After warmup, the current classifier advances the orbit and scans candidate lags from period 1 through maxPeriod. For points that remain unresolved for most of the budget, the dominant comparison count is approximately:

orbit steps × candidate lags ≈ O(maxIterations × maxPeriod) per pixel

That makes the Balanced worst case roughly 512 × 32 lag comparisons and Detailed roughly 1024 × 64, before verification and output work. The difficult screenshots supplied during diagnosis showed classification dominating elapsed time across multiple views rather than one isolated UI action.

Secondary costs

  • Per-pixel Complex, result, and evidence allocations.
  • Static horizontal bands despite spatially uneven orbit difficulty.
  • Per-pixel Complex, result, and evidence allocations also drive scavenger GC during renders: pause time and classifyMs variance grow, and that same variance weakens the paired-interval gates in section 9.
  • Row yields use nested setTimeout(0), which browsers clamp to a 4 ms minimum after five nesting levels (the HTML timer-nesting rule); part of the current yield budget is timer policy, not compute.
  • Supervisor plus stable child semantic buffers: roughly 42 MiB at 1024² before RGBA; if coarse buffers overlap in lifetime, a conservative semantic peak is roughly 63 MiB before Wasm arenas and RGBA.
  • Full-frame recoloring/assembly risks if dynamic subtiles are implemented naively.

Measurement defects

  • Cached/replayed frames can expose timing copied from the original request.
  • classifyMs is not semantically equivalent in serial and nested-worker paths.
  • Marks and long-task observation can accumulate.
  • Easy default-view medians conceal hard interior tails.

Evidence available before Phase 2

  • Easy/default tiled 1024² measurements were approximately 0.90 s median in Chrome and 1.24 s in Firefox on the earlier target environment.
  • Hard supplied period-4 views were approximately 11–23 s, with classification accounting for most of the time.
  • The three hard-view anchors to retain in the corpus are approximately: 126× at (−0.158902249, −1.034028), 609× at (−1.94130973, −0.0000974722949), and 13× at (0.305376533, 0.552677981).
  • Provenance: these values come from the Phase 0/1 diagnosis records under evidence/ and the difficult-screenshot review, recorded on an earlier target environment. They are not directly comparable with docs/PHASE0_BENCHMARK.md, which reports a ≈1.96 s Chrome stable median at 1024² on different hardware; figures from different environments must not be mixed.
Interpretation limit. These values motivate the work but are not Phase 2 release baselines. Hardware, browser builds, viewport size, warmup, cache state, and measurement semantics must be recorded again by the benchmark contract below.

3. Correctness and product invariants

  1. Candidate generation is not proof. Approximate cycle checkpoints, catalog proximity, Newton correction, low-period algebraic relations, and spatial indexes may propose a period/cycle only.
  2. One verifier decides. Every attracting result passes the same finite-value, primitive-period, closure, residual, and attraction checks regardless of source or backend.
  3. Primitive period is explicit. A period p candidate must be reduced against every proper divisor of p; a multiple of a smaller cycle must never be emitted as primitive.
  4. Uncertainty stays visible. Numerical ambiguity, budget exhaustion, unsupported opportunistic periods, or verifier disagreement produce unresolved/fallback, not a confident class.
  5. Cache changes speed, never truth. Disabling identity, atlas, session, or semantic caches cannot alter a completed semantic result.
  6. Backend parity. Scalar TypeScript, scalar Wasm, and SIMD Wasm must agree under the same tolerance policy and semantic fixtures.
  7. Responsiveness remains bounded. Coarse preview, cancellation, and worker yields remain functional; a faster total render may not create long uninterruptible kernels.
  8. Systematic and opportunistic claims differ. Quality describes the guaranteed search budget; independently found higher periods may be displayed only after verification and with origin metadata.

Semantic compatibility contract

Phase 2 is conservative but not required to be bit-identical to the legacy classifier. Categorical status and primitive period are judged against independent high-precision fixtures and deterministic stratified holdouts. A changed legacy answer may ship only when the oracle supports the change. False attracting results and wrong primitive periods are release blockers; numerically ambiguous cases remain unresolved. Multiplier magnitude, angle, and κ are compared with declared tolerances rather than cross-browser binary hashes. At superattracting points |λ| = 0 and κ is infinite: comparisons treat such points by identity (both sides |λ| = 0) rather than by arithmetic on infinities, and that rule is versioned with the tolerance policy.

Common numerical verifier

For fc(z) = z² + c and a proposed period p, the verifier must:

  1. reject non-finite state, residual, or derivative values;
  2. check fcp(z) − z against a scale-aware residual tolerance;
  3. test every proper divisor d of p and reduce if closure is already present;
  4. compute λ = (fcp)′(z) and require |λ| < 1 − margin;
  5. near tolerance boundaries, require a second closure/contraction check or return unresolved;
  6. emit period, |λ|, arg λ, κ, evidence code, and verifier revision.

Use distinct thresholds τcandidate > τaccept and a three-way divisor policy: accept closure below a scaled acceptance bound; exclude a smaller divisor only above a larger separation bound; treat the gap as ambiguous. Residual tolerance, binary64 error allowance, attraction margin, and scale normalization are versioned policy, exercised against higher-precision fixtures, and documented in PERFORMANCE-MATHEMATICS.md. A repeated check using the same arithmetic is consistency evidence, not certification.

4. Target classifier architecture

  1. Cheap closed-form pathsMain-cardioid period 1 and period-2 bulb tests evaluated in binary64; escaped points leave early. These tests already exist in src/domain/orbit.ts (analyticInterior); Phase 2 preserves them and migrates their acceptance to the versioned verifier policy (scale-aware tolerance, attraction margin) instead of reimplementing them.
  2. Orbit + checkpointAdvance primitive scalar or SIMD lanes and maintain the approximate checkpoint schedule defined below.
  3. Candidate queuesCombine checkpoint lag, catalog/chart seed, or optional p3/p4 relation into same-period work queues.
  4. Verify or fall backRun the common verifier; emit semantics only on success, otherwise continue, fall back, or remain unresolved.
Brent cycle detection, defined at first use. In Richard Brent’s 1980 cycle-detection algorithm, a sequence xn+1 = f(xn) is searched for an exact repetition using O(1) stored states. One state advances every step while a stationary checkpoint is retained. After 1, 2, 4, 8, … advances without equality, the current state becomes the new checkpoint and the interval doubles. Exact equality yields a cycle-length candidate with O(μ + L) function evaluations, where μ is the nonrepeating prefix length and L is the cycle length. This document reserves λ for the complex multiplier.

This project borrows only Brent’s exponentially spaced checkpoint schedule. For the complex orbit zn+1 = zn² + c, an attracting orbit converges asymptotically and normally does not repeat exactly in binary64. At each step the kernel compares the current zn with the retained checkpoint zk. When |zn − zk| falls below the permissive threshold τcandidate, the lag q = n − k is proposed as a possible period. When the current checkpoint interval is exhausted, the kernel stores the current state as the next checkpoint, doubles the interval, and continues.

That is why the plan calls the method Brent-inspired approximate checkpointing, not Brent cycle detection: approximate proximity destroys Brent’s exact finite-state guarantee. A hit may propose the primitive period p, a multiple kp, or an accidental near-return. It never classifies a pixel. Rejected candidates are capped per pixel; the common verifier tests closure with the stricter τaccept, reduces proper divisors, recomputes λ, and falls back when exclusion is ambiguous. The implementation specification must freeze the checkpoint update schedule, both tolerances, rejection budget, maximum detection delay, and fallback behavior before benchmarking.

Near weak attraction, required work behaves roughly as n ≈ ntransient + p · log(τ/e0) / log|λ|, which grows without bound as |λ| → 1.

Therefore a maximum period does not guarantee detection without an adequate iteration and verification budget. Changing 32 to 12, 24, 48, or 64 is not intrinsically SIMD-friendly; speed comes from removing the all-lag scan and batching same-stage/same-period work.

Alternative and complementary detection schedules

The power-of-two checkpoint schedule is one member of a family; two relatives must be A/B-tested against it behind the legacy differential flag before any schedule is frozen:

  • Convergence-triggered single scan. Per iteration, compare |zn − zn−1| against a scale-aware step gate; only when it fires, run one full lag scan from the current state. Failed scans re-arm the trigger at a quarter of the previous threshold, capped per pixel. Cost is one gate per iteration plus a bounded number of O(maxPeriod) scans, and the single scan finds the minimal lag directly, avoiding checkpoint multiple-of-p churn and the rejection-retry ambiguity.
  • Staggered harmonic lag testing. Test lag p only every p-th iteration. Average comparisons per iteration fall from maxPeriod to ≈ H(maxPeriod) ≈ 4 at 32, with detection delayed by less than p iterations. This is the near-zero-risk fallback if checkpoint or trigger sensitivity regresses on the holdouts.
  • Exhaustion scan. When a schedule-based kernel ends its orbit budget unresolved, one final full lag scan recovers most missed detections at O(maxPeriod) cost per affected pixel. It is on by default for the conditional schedules and its measured value is reported separately.

Two implementation rules follow. First, rejection-retry semantics must be frozen explicitly: a checkpoint or trigger candidate that fails verification is retested against the same retained state after a doubling re-arm gap, not dropped through a full interval. Second, gate C requires a matched-budget detection analysis on the stratified holdouts — per-pixel detected-period deltas versus the legacy scan at equal iteration budgets — so detection delay is visible as a distribution, not only as an aggregate unresolved-rate delta.

Renderer-path efficiency details

Small, individually gated changes ride along the required workstreams; each is measured and none is assumed:

  • Zero-copy output. Pre-slice the supervisor semantic frame and hand each tile worker transferable views it classifies into directly, eliminating per-band allocations and the merge memcpy (~21 MiB per 1024² stable frame). Part of E's reusable-buffer deliverable; measured via mergeCpuMs and allocation counts.
  • Yield mechanism. Replace nested setTimeout(0) yields with MessageChannel ports. Browsers clamp nested timers to a 4 ms minimum after five nestings, so the current yield budget is partly timer policy; measured via cancel-to-child-quiescence and bandElapsedMs minus bandComputeMs.
  • Center-out band scheduling. Order static or microbands from the viewport center outward so mid-screen presents first — a perceived-latency change with zero throughput cost, gated on time-to-first-50%-rows.
  • Packed status+period output. Pack status and primitive period into one Uint32 (period ≤ 2²⁴), merging two stores into one per pixel and saving ~5% of semantic-frame bytes (20% of the status+period fields) plus the corresponding copy traffic; adopt only together with zero-copy.
  • Ring-buffer micro-costs. If the legacy ring survives anywhere: power-of-two capacity with mask indexing, no out-of-bounds sentinel reads, and the warmup-invariant lag bound hoisted out of the inner loop.

Four-layer catalog architecture

  1. Curated identity layer. Keep the existing 11 named centers for navigation, tests, explanations, and stable IDs.
  2. Exhaustive period-12 core (ships only with F). Compact center/period/index data always available to workers; budget ≤256 KiB encoded and ≤512 KiB resident. The earlier 130–200 KiB estimate is a hypothesis until the binary schema is fixed.
  3. Demand-loaded performance patches (ship only with F). Content-hashed, cancellable 32–128 KiB compressed shards keyed by a static spatial index. A patch may include cycle seed, parameter derivative, conservative attempt region, residual/error bound, generator revision, and verifier revision.
  4. Ephemeral session atlas. Worker-local 8–16 MiB LRU of successfully verified local cycles. Session data accelerates revisits but is never silently promoted to shipped catalog truth.

Center locations and seed applicability bounds are separate indexes: a deeply zoomed component may have its center far offscreen. The curated 11 remain direct navigation controls and are the only shipped markers in the required scope. If F ships, dynamic results use deterministic clustering plus an accessible search/list and a roving-focus composite, targeting at most 20–30 simultaneous interactive markers; every suppressed item remains reachable by search/list, selected identity is retained, and shard arrival does not reorder current keyboard focus.

Deterministic stable and refined frames. Any bundled core that ships participates in the initial stable computation. A render snapshots algorithm, policy, catalog, and loaded seed-set (including session-atlas) revisions at start; arriving shards never mutate it in flight. Optional shards may later change unresolved to a verified opportunistic result only through an explicit refined frame with evidence. Cache provenance includes all snapshot revisions. Missing, corrupt, slow, or canceled shards fall back without blocking stable rendering.

Offline generation and independent validation

Let P1(c)=c and Pn+1(c)=Pn(c)²+c, so Pn(c)=fcn(0). The production generator constructs the exact-period critical-orbit center factor Hn(c)=Pn(c)/∏d|n,d<nHd(c) using exact integer-polynomial division. This avoids conflating center factors with generic formal-period dynatomic polynomials.

The existing dense-polynomial/Durand–Kerner generator is retained only as a low-period cross-check. The implementation milestone must select one hermetic, pinned primary toolchain (container, versions, precision escalation, licenses, deterministic rounding) based on FLINT/Arb-class exact and ball arithmetic, with MPSolve-class solving as an independent cross-check where practical. A menu of unpinned tools is not an accepted generator design.

  • Verify expected primitive-period counts for each p ≤ 12.
  • Re-run at higher precision and bound root movement.
  • Check center residual, proper-divisor exclusion, and conjugate closure.
  • Require disjoint root enclosures, correct real-root handling, and no duplicate roots.
  • Assign deterministic ordering, stable IDs, content hashes, tool versions, precision, and tolerance metadata.
  • Cross-check low periods with an independent method; do not let the runtime file be its own oracle.
  • Record the offline compute budget: degree-2048 ball root isolation is feasible but not free; the runbook states expected wall time and CI smoke policy (recount and spot-check, not per-pull-request regeneration).

How Newton feeds the SIMD-friendly design

For a proposed period p, define:

Fp(z,c) = fcp(z) − z    and    ∂Fp/∂z = λ − 1.

Along an orbit, the parameter derivative obeys the inexpensive recurrence:

Bj+1 = 2zjBj + 1,   B0 = 0,   so locally   dz*/dc = B/(1 − λ).

A catalog entry or verified neighboring pixel predicts a cycle point at the new parameter. The seed must retain a defined cycle phase; multiplier is phase-invariant but the Newton seed is not. Eligible candidates are bucketed by period, then a small bounded number of Newton corrections can run in lockstep across independent pixels. This is more SIMD-friendly than mixing arbitrary periods in one lane group because every lane performs the same p-step recurrence. Newton may converge to a lower-period or repelling root; the verifier remains separate.

Conditioning guard. The predictor becomes ill-conditioned as λ → 1, especially near parabolic boundaries. Conservative attempt regions, denominator thresholds, bounded corrections, and immediate fallback are mandatory. Attempt regions are derived from the multiplier map (the Douady–Hubbard λ-isomorphism) rather than tuned constants: a seed is eligible when its first-order displacement estimate |B|·|Δc|/|1 − λ| stays inside the versioned guard, which degrades exactly as λ → 1. At superattracting centers λ = 0, Koenigs linearization is not the right local coordinate; Böttcher-type coordinates are the adjacent theory, but they remain research-only here.

Low-period algebra and partial closed forms

The full raster problem has no useful global closed form, but several subproblems do:

  • Main-cardioid membership/period-1 and period-2 bulb membership use exact mathematical formulas evaluated in binary64; ambiguity remains a numerical concern.
  • Proper-divisor sets, expected exact-period counts, and some low-period multiplier relations can be generated exactly offline.
  • For periods 3 and 4, low-degree relations Qp(c,λ)=0 may generate candidates faster than long orbit scans in selected regions; branch selection and numerical conditioning remain iterative verification problems.
  • Affine viewport mapping, pixel coordinates, and palette transforms are closed-form housekeeping and should stay outside the hot inner recurrence.

Any radical or polynomial solution is evaluated as a floating candidate and passed through the same verifier. “Closed form” does not waive numerical branch, cancellation, or residual analysis.

Spatial subdivision

A Morton/quadtree representation is useful immediately for catalog sharding, demand loading, viewport queries, scheduling, cache locality, and prioritization. It is not safe evidence that all pixels in a rectangle share a semantic result. Corners and edges can agree while the interior contains fine Mandelbrot structure.

Stable block skipping is a separate research track requiring a parametric rigorous enclosure method—such as interval arithmetic plus a Krawczyk-style test—that proves escape or existence/uniqueness, exact period, and attraction uniformly over the whole parameter block. Even a certified uniform period does not justify one multiplier color: λ(c), its angle, and κ vary, so color fill also needs a rigorous semantic/palette variation bound. Approximate subdivision may drive only the coarse preview, and exterior/interior distance estimation — both derived from the same parameter-derivative recurrence Bj+1 = 2zjBj + 1 — is its concrete tool: exterior distance bounds block-fill escaped regions and interior bounds block-fill within verified components, at preview-only fidelity.

7. Wasm SIMD design for Chrome and Firefox

Compatibility conclusion. Fixed-width WebAssembly SIMD is available in modern Chrome and Firefox, including f64x2. The plan still feature-detects the actual module and preserves the TypeScript scalar path because browser support alone does not guarantee speed, successful instantiation, or parity on every target environment.

Kernel shape

  • A small pinned Rust wasm32-unknown-unknown crate using stable Wasm SIMD intrinsics; no relaxed SIMD and no fast-math assumptions.
  • The SIMD module is a separate artifact from any scalar Wasm module. Unsupported engines can reject SIMD at validation time, so TypeScript—not code inside that module—is the mandatory fallback.
  • Scalar Wasm is a gated stepping stone, not a throwaway: it ships independently if it clears ≥1.2× the staged TypeScript classifier with parity, and SIMD Wasm must then beat scalar Wasm by its own ≥1.20× to ship.
  • One private Wasm instance and pre-sized aligned arena per tile worker; no shared linear memory, Wasm threads, SharedArrayBuffer, or cross-origin-isolation dependency.
  • Compile the shipping module once in the supervisor and structured-clone the compiled WebAssembly.Module to child workers; instantiate with private memory per child.
  • Structure-of-arrays inputs: real and imaginary components packed separately. Independent pixels occupy lanes.
  • Same-stage and, for Newton/verification, same-period queues reduce divergence. Completed lanes are masked; remaining items refill only at safe batch boundaries.
  • Scalar odd tail and scalar fallback for small or badly divergent queues.
  • No JavaScript↔Wasm crossing per pixel; batches amortize calls and copies.
  • Never await Wasm before coarse presentation. If concurrent compilation measurably harms coarse latency, start it after coarse and let the first stable pass use TypeScript.
  • Feature and failure handling covers asset/MIME/CSP, validation, compile, link, instantiate, worker start, and runtime trap. The deployment CSP is reviewed explicitly; it is not weakened silently.
  • Classify a microband inside Wasm, copy its output once to a recyclable transferable buffer, and avoid returning private Wasm memory directly.

8. Low-overhead observability contract

The plan does not recommend per-pixel tracing. Observability is split into a tiny always-on request summary and an explicitly enabled diagnostic mode. Counters are accumulated in preallocated worker-local structures and reported once per band or final frame.

Always-on: bounded local ring

Retain the last 32 request summaries, no remote telemetry, no per-pixel timers, and no unbounded User Timing marks. A requestId identifies UI intent; a computeId identifies actual semantic work and survives rebinding/replay. Environment metadata is stored once per session.

RenderTrace {
  requestId, computeId?, viewKeyHash, width, height, profile,
  backend, workerCount, outcome, frames[],
  cancelAckMs?, childQuiescenceMs?,
  mainLongTaskCount, maxMainLongTaskMs
}

FrameTrace {
  stage: "coarse" | "stable" | "refined",
  source: "computed" | "semantic-cache" | "inflight-replay" | "recolor",
  originComputeId?, requestToPresentMs, workerWallMs?, colorizeMs,
  maxBandElapsedMs?, imbalanceRatio?, mergeCpuMs?
}

requestToPresentMs ends at the next presentation opportunity after image upload; it is not proof of physical paint. workerWallMs spans worker/supervisor start through complete semantic assembly. bandComputeMs excludes explicit yields, while bandElapsedMs includes them. Aggregate compute is diagnostic and is never called wall or CPU time. Cache, replay, and recolor frames carry fresh delivery timing with compute fields absent, never zero or copied.

Cancellation has two meanings: cancelAckMs for user-visible acknowledgment and childQuiescenceMs for all affected children stopping or the pool resetting. Main-thread Long Task data is summarized as count/max; it says nothing about worker long tasks.

Opt-in: ?perf=1 or benchmark mode

Add integer counters only where they answer a decision:

  • final classification totals and systematic/opportunistic period buckets;
  • analytic/checkpoint/catalog/chart/algebraic/fallback path hits;
  • candidate proposals, verifier rejects by reason, Newton corrections, and fallback transitions;
  • sampled orbit-iteration histogram rather than every pixel’s history;
  • catalog shard load/hit, worker imbalance, SIMD lane utilization, packing/copy time.
Overhead budgets. Normal mode has no hot-loop instrumentation branch, retains ≤64 KiB, and averages ≤0.2 ms recorder work per frame — enforced per render, since 0.2 ms is below reliable single-frame attribution; paired end-to-end non-inferiority allows at most 2% overhead. Opt-in diagnostics allow at most 5%. The classifier selects lean or instrumented functions outside the raster loop, samples iteration histograms deterministically (for example 1/1024 pixels), and transfers one compact summary per band. If a metric exceeds the budget, move it to an offline experiment.

  • Correctness suite: independent high-precision point and raster oracles for status, primitive period, residual, multiplier, and expected ambiguity.

The screenshot-derived hard views are seeds for exact records, not themselves the records. Protocol

  1. Run current stable branded Chrome and Firefox—not only automation-bundled engines—on the declared target laptop class, with CPU, cores, RAM, OS/kernel, exact browser build, power/battery mode, headed state, device-pixel ratio, viewport, build and algorithm/catalog revisions, worker count, and backend recorded. DevTools is closed. The harness runs the production bundle (vite build + vite preview), never the dev server; build mode is part of the recorded environment.
  2. Use shipping viewport 1024×640 plus square 768² and 1024² diagnostic rasters.
  3. Separate cold page/process, cold worker/Wasm compile, cold catalog shard, cold semantic cache, warm code/pool/catalog, computed, cache, replay, and recolor cases.
  4. Use 9–11 paired repetitions for full-corpus median screening, at least 21 paired repetitions for release-gate cases, at least 40 observations for any reported p95, and at least 50 interactions per cancellation distribution. Store every sample.
  5. Report median, median absolute deviation, paired interval, min/max, and p95 only with adequate samples. Hash discrete status/period fields with documented byte order; compare continuous evidence by tolerances and distributions.
  6. Paired intervals are 95% bias-corrected accelerated (BCa) bootstrap intervals on paired per-case differences (log-ratios for speed ratios), computed from stored paired samples with the resampling seed recorded in the manifest. Per-case regression caps are evaluated per case without family-wise adjustment; the expected count of chance flags across the corpus is reported alongside each gate result.
  7. Declare a machine-time envelope in the manifest: 21 paired release-gate repetitions over 11–23 s hard cases imply hours per browser per variant, so the manifest distinguishes screening cases (9–11 reps) from release-gate cases (21+) and a full gate run targets ≤4 h per browser per variant on the declared target hardware.
  8. Randomize or alternate baseline/candidate order to reduce thermal and temporal bias. A single run may not establish a ship claim.
  9. Keep benchmark assertions out of ordinary wall-clock CI. CI validates semantics, schemas, deterministic fixtures, fallbacks, and smoke budgets; controlled evidence runs support performance decisions.

Program-level success

  • Correctness dominates speed: zero false attracting results and zero wrong primitive periods against oracle/holdout evidence.
  • Absolute product targets: coarse and stable latency budgets are frozen after the clean Stage A target-hardware baseline and take precedence over arbitrary ratios. An absolute user-facing hard-view budget is declared with them (for example, worst declared hard view at Detailed on target hardware); if the 2× exit criterion cannot meet that budget, closure requires the conditional workstreams' dispositions plus an explicit product decision, not silent acceptance of a still-slow view.
  • Cumulative target: aim for at least 3× on the predefined hard-corpus geometric/weighted aggregate; this is a program target, not a reason to ship a failed experiment.
  • Per-PR admission: use each workstream’s stated benefit gate. Reject a regression when the paired median exceeds max(5%, 20 ms) and its paired interval excludes zero; both browsers pass independently.
  • Semantic change: unresolved-rate increase ≤0.1 percentage point unless supported by the oracle and explicitly approved; legacy mismatch alone is not failure.

11. Delivery sequence and dependency logic

  1. Step 0 — Proof-of-concept harness (poc/performance/; M). Directional, oracle-checked variant evidence (schedules, DE period guessing, neighbor-informed lag ordering, bounded exhaustion extension, transplantation, trap, conjugate-mirror parity, renderer-path details) that feeds PR 1's corpus and gate choices. Specified at revision 2; not yet landed as of revision 4 — it is a Phase 2 entry deliverable and must precede PR 1.
  2. PR 1 — Corpus, timing, and bounded observability (M). Establish truth before optimizing it.
  3. PR 2 — Allocation-free scalar kernel (S–M). Remove obvious overhead with minimal algorithm change.
  4. PR 3 — Common verifier and semantic oracle (M). Freeze candidate acceptance before adding candidate sources.
  5. PR 4 — Checkpoint candidates (M–L). Attack the O(iterations × periods) scan behind a legacy differential flag.
  6. PR 5 — Period policy buckets and product language (S). Separate systematic cost from opportunistic verified discoveries.
  7. Conditional PR — Conjugate symmetry mirroring (workstream M; size S–M). Dispatch-level row dedup for viewports intersecting their conjugate; cheap and multiplicative with every backend and schedule, so it goes first among the conditionals.
  8. Conditional PR — Cross-profile semantic carryover (workstream O; size S–M). Carry budget-independent (escaped, verified-attracting) pixels across profile upgrades at an unchanged viewport; recompute unresolved only.
  9. Conditional PR — Cost-weighted static banding (workstream N; size S). Split stable bands by coarse-pass estimated cost instead of equal height; attempted before E, and meeting E's bar kills E with retained evidence.
  10. Conditional PR — Dynamic microbands (workstream E; size M). Attempt only on residual skew that N demonstrably leaves; emit one stable frame first.
  11. Conditional PR — Catalog/session/adjacent-pixel Newton candidates (transplantation) (M). Use same-period queues and common verification; the raster-coherent seed source reaches zooms the catalog cannot.
  12. Conditional PR — Reproducible period-12 generator and compact core (workstream F; L — the largest single item in this plan). Attempt only if C and the Newton PoC quantify seed value checkpoint detection alone cannot provide; ship data only after independent validation. Default disposition is deferral to a product (atlas) phase.
  13. Conditional PR — Dynamic catalog LOD and accessibility (M). Ships only together with F; otherwise the curated 11 keep their existing Phase 1 validation.
  14. Conditional PR — p3/p4 algebraic path (M). Merge only if its targeted or weighted gate is met.
  15. Conditional PR — Wasm SIMD backend (L). Optimize the stable algorithm and compare TypeScript, scalar Wasm, and SIMD Wasm; scalar Wasm may ship first under its own gate.
  16. Conditional PR — Pool sizing, zero-copy output, yield mechanism, band order (S–M). Workstream K plus the renderer-path efficiency details; one measured bundle or separate small PRs.
  17. Research track — Rigorous subdivision (open-ended). Separate from release until a certified whole-block enclosure exists.
  18. Research track — Trap-radius early accept (open-ended). Workstream L; oracle-gated, research-only.

Sizes are relative single-engineer focused-effort estimates at project scale (S < 1 week, M ≈ 1–3 weeks, L > 3 weeks). They order risk and sequencing; they are not commitments.

Sequencing rationale. SIMD is intentionally late. A vectorized O(iterations × periods) algorithm can still waste most of its work; changing the scalar algorithm first produces value on every browser and exposes homogeneous work queues that SIMD can exploit.

12. Risks, kill gates, and explicit exclusions

Highest-risk assumptions

  • Approximate checkpoint candidates preserve detection sensitivity after removing the full lag scan.
  • (Conditional, F only — off the closeout critical path after the rev 3 demotion) Period-12 proximity predicts enough useful cycles at the product zoom ceiling.
  • Newton attempts stay well-conditioned often enough to repay bucketing and verification.
  • Wasm packing/copy and lane divergence do not erase f64x2 gains in Firefox.
  • Dynamic scheduling helps hard views without recolor/message overhead hurting common cases.
  • Adjacent-pixel transplantation and its attempt guard degrade gracefully near parabolic boundaries (|B|·|Δc| blows up) and do not shift attraction/unresolved boundaries.
  • Schedule-based detection (checkpoint or trigger) does not push detection past the iteration budget on weakly attracting pixels.
  • Conjugate-mirror coordinate construction stays inside the versioned tolerance policy; a sub-ulp sampling offset must not flip boundary pixels against the oracle.
  • The coarse-pass cost signal predicts stable-pass cost well enough for N to balance bands; otherwise E remains the fallback.

Mitigations

  • Legacy differential backend and invariant fixtures.
  • Per-case gates and conservative attempt regions.
  • Independent generator validation and content hashes.
  • Backend feature/self-tests and scalar fallback.
  • Measure skew first; throttle assembly; cache final stable frames only.
  • PoC differential runs and double-double oracle adjudication before any schedule or seed policy is frozen; matched-budget detection analysis in gate C.

Explicitly out of scope

  • Reopening Phase 1 closeout absent a regression.
  • Changing the product zoom ceiling or adopting perturbation/deep-zoom arithmetic in this phase.
  • WebGPU as the default solution; it remains a separately gated architecture decision.
  • Wasm threads, SharedArrayBuffer, or COOP/COEP deployment changes.
  • Remote performance telemetry or per-pixel production tracing.
  • Rendering all 4,016 catalog entries as DOM controls.
  • Using a catalog lookup, Newton convergence, multiplier relation, or matching subdivision samples as proof without the common verifier.
  • f32/f64 mixed-precision pre-passes for exterior pixels: they accelerate only exterior-heavy (already fast) views and complicate backend parity.
  • Incremental per-pixel coordinate stepping and other sub-ulp coordinate shortcuts; per-pixel exact affine math stays.
  • Hand-tuned micro-fixes with no hot-loop cost (for example Math.hypot outside candidate paths) — evaluated and rejected.
  • Scalar Wasm as a required deliverable: it is a gated stepping stone under workstream I, not an obligation.

Final Phase 2 exit criteria

  • Required workstreams meet their individual gates in both stable browsers; every conditional experiment has a recorded accept/reject disposition.
  • The required checkpoint path achieves at least 2× on declared worst hard cases and the frozen absolute latency budgets. The 3× integrated hard-corpus goal remains a program target, not a reason to hold closure open indefinitely if optional experiments fail honestly.
  • Categorical results satisfy the high-precision oracle/holdout contract; reviewed conservative changes from legacy output are documented.
  • If F ships, the dynamic catalog UI passes keyboard, screen-reader, focus, clustering, and marker-cap validation; otherwise the curated layer's existing Phase 1 accessibility validation stands.
  • Mathematics, requirements, ADRs, implementation, generator, evidence, and SysML views agree.
  • Every experiment that did not ship is recorded with its measurements and kill rationale.
